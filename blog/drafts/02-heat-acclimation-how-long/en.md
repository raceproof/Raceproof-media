How long does heat acclimation take?

<!-- answer -->
Plan on 1 to 2 weeks of repeated sessions in the heat. That's what a 2015 expert consensus recommends, and a meta-analysis of 96 articles found the biggest adaptations come after more than 14 days. The first changes show up sooner: in a small trial, 5 days of hot rides lowered heart rate and raised plasma volume, and in another, sweat sodium started dropping from day 3.

<!-- body -->
## What do the experts recommend?

The [Racinais et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25943653/) consensus statement, written by 17 authors, calls heat acclimatisation the most important step to reduce strain and protect performance in a hot race. It should be repeated exercise in the heat over 1 to 2 weeks. It's expert consensus, not new data, aimed mainly at elite events.

A meta-analysis by [Tyler et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27106556/) pooled 96 articles on heat adaptation. The most common block lasted 7 to 14 days. Heat adaptation had a moderate benefit on performance in the heat whatever the plan, and longer blocks worked better: under 14 days brings many benefits, but adaptations are greatest beyond 14 days. Methods varied widely, so read it as a direction, not a dose.

## How fast do the first changes show up?

In [Snape et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0345346), 14 endurance-trained men were randomized to 5 days of 90-minute rides either in the heat (32.2°C, 69.7% humidity, core temperature pushed to 38.5°C or more) or at 21.3°C. In the heat group, heart rate fell 8 bpm, plasma volume rose 7.3%, sweat loss rose 500 mL and peak effort rating fell 2 points. It's a small lab study with 7 men per group and no performance test, and the authors declared no competing interests.

A meta-analysis of heat decay, [Daanen et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29129022/), agrees: 5 or more heat days are enough for adaptations in heart rate and core temperature.

Sweat adapts on its own schedule. In [Klous et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33077118/), 8 people cycled in the heat (33°C, 65% humidity) for 10 days in a row. Sweat sodium began to fall from day 3, but sweat rate only rose from day 7 to 8. By day 10, local sweat rate was up 58% on the arm and 36% on the back, and sweat sodium had fallen to about 60% of its day 1 level. It's a small lab study using skin patches.

## Does it make you faster in the heat?

In a controlled trial, yes. In [Zurawlew et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26661992/), 17 men who weren't used to heat ran 40 minutes at 18°C each day for 6 days, then sat for 40 minutes in water at 40°C (10 men) or a neutral 34°C (7 men). After the hot baths, their 5 km treadmill time trial in 33°C improved 4.9%, with no change at 18°C. The control group didn't change.

It's a small group and a 5 km lab test, not a long race. The gain was specific to the heat.

## Do sauna and hot baths count?

Small studies suggest they do. In an observational study, [Stanley et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25432420/), 7 well-trained male cyclists added 30 minutes of sauna at 87°C after training for 10 days. Plasma volume peaked after four sessions, at 17.8% above baseline. No control group, no performance test, and a debated statistics method.

Longer use may do something else. In [Jenkins et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1113/JP289874), 10 well-trained runners (9 men) added five 45-minute hot baths a week at 40°C or hotter for 5 weeks. VO2max rose 2.7 mL/kg/min and blood volume 284 mL against a control period. That's an aerobic gain, separate from getting ready for a hot race, in a small study of mostly men.

Hot baths and sauna aren't for everyone. If you have a heart condition, high or low blood pressure, take blood pressure medication, are pregnant, or have fainted or had heat illness before, talk to your doctor before trying them.

## How long does heat acclimation last?

[Daanen et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29129022/) found that about 2.5% of the adaptations are lost for every day without heat, and end-of-exercise heart rate adaptation fell 2.3% per day. In Klous et al., the 4 people followed up kept part of their adaptations after 28 days, and 5 days of heat again may restore them. Both rely mostly on lab studies.

## What it means for your race

**Marathon.** If your race is usually cool, heat is a low priority. If it's often hot, the research points to 1 to 2 weeks of repeated heat sessions before it, not a single hot long run.

**Ultra.** In a survey at the 2025 Western States 100, [Mougin et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42303557/) found 90% of runners did heat preparation, and over 70% reported more than 10 days or exposures. It's a self-reported survey at one race, so it shows what runners did, not what worked.

**Triathlon.** Most of the trials above used indoor cycling, so a ride in the heat is a real heat session. Your sweat changes as you adapt, so re-check your sweat rate after a heat block.

**When to start.** Heat has its own window: build it 6 to 2 weeks before your race. It takes about 10 to 14 days of heat sessions, then some heat exposure to race day keeps it, since gains fade within a few weeks. [See your own timeline: try the demo](https://raceproof.ai/demo).

## Our take

*This is our opinion, not a study finding.* Count heat in days, not workouts. Aim for 10 to 14 days of heat sessions inside the window 6 to 2 weeks out, then keep one or two short sessions a week until race day. Five days is a fair start if that's all you have. A hot bath or sauna after training adds heat without adding miles, built up slowly and never alone. Heat done 20 weeks out will mostly be gone by race day.

<!-- faq -->
### Is 5 days of heat training enough?
It's a start. In [Snape et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0345346), 5 days lowered heart rate by 8 bpm and raised plasma volume 7.3% in 7 trained men. Fuller adaptation takes 1 to 2 weeks ([Racinais et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25943653/)).

### Does a sauna after running count as heat training?
In small studies, yes. Four post-training sauna sessions raised plasma volume 17.8% in 7 cyclists ([Stanley et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25432420/)), and 6 days of hot baths after runs improved a 5 km test in the heat by 4.9% ([Zurawlew et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26661992/)). If you have a heart or blood pressure condition or are pregnant, talk to your doctor first.

### How quickly do I lose heat acclimation?
About 2.5% of the adaptations are lost per day without heat ([Daanen et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29129022/)). In a small study, part of them was still there after 28 days ([Klous et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33077118/)).

### Should I re-test my sweat rate after heat training?
For a hot race, yes. After 10 days of heat, local sweat rate rose 36 to 58% and sweat sodium fell to about 60% of day 1 in 8 people ([Klous et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33077118/)). A test from before your heat block may overstate sodium loss and understate your sweat rate.
