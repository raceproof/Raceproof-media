How did Vincent Luis prepare for the heat at Kona?

Vincent Luis won the 2026 IRONMAN World Championship in Kona in 7:47:27, on a day that reached 88°F at the airport. By his own account, he started heat work with hot baths and sauna in January and February, added easy rides and runs in the heat before IRONMAN Texas in April, and 3 weeks out was doing IRONMAN-pace sessions in a heat chamber at 32°C and 80% humidity. On race day he said he took about 145 g of carbs an hour on the bike and about 100 g an hour on the run.

One elite athlete (n = 1). His prep numbers are self-reported, from interviews after the race. He has no connection to Raceproof.

## What did Kona ask of him?

Official IRONMAN results: 1st in 7:47:27, with a 48:31 swim, a 4:15:42 bike and a 2:38:36 run. Gustav Iden was 2nd in 7:49:02, 1:35 back. Luis came off the bike sixth, 6:04 behind Magnus Ditlev (Tri247), and took the lead at the mile 21 aid station (Slowtwitch race report).

The heat and humidity were the main demand. At Kona International Airport (PHKO) the high was 88°F. At 6:53 am it was already 79°F with 88% humidity, and from 6 am to 5 pm humidity ran from 61% to 88%. The airport isn't on the course. IRONMAN's course page lists a 2026 high of 88°F (31°C) and average water of 79°F (26°C).

## How did he train for the heat?

In his words, in stages:

- **Passive heat first.** "Since January February I really started with passive [heat], so hot bath, sauna and everything" ([All-In Triathlon](https://youtu.be/TXpXTfYTn28), uploaded Oct 12, 2026; the episode is sponsored by Precision Fuel & Hydration).
- **Active heat before Texas.** Before IRONMAN Texas in April, he added "easy rides, easy runs" in the heat (same interview). He said he raced Texas "for obvious reasons with early qualifying, racing a bit in the heat and humidity" ([ProTriNews](https://youtu.be/6Mpl1odkY5g), recorded Oct 11, 2026).
- **Race pace in the heat chamber.** "3 weeks out of Kona I could do Iron Man pace sessions in the heat chamber at 32 degrees and 80% humidity," running efforts of about 8 minutes at 3:30 per km at a heart rate of 150, while also at altitude (All-In Triathlon).
- **Heat and altitude together.** He arrived in Font-Romeu "maybe 10 to 12 weeks before the race" and did "altitude training and heat training... both in the heat chamber at altitude" ([The Triathlon Hour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uY61pcBHsyQ), Oct 18, 2026).
- **Timing.** He skipped the race in Nice because "four weeks out of the race... is the exact moment you want to clock big weeks and you want to do the heat prep" (The Triathlon Hour).

He also checked his core temperature. On a day of almost 40°C, his core temperature rose to about 39.5°C during 5 x 5 minutes at threshold, by his own estimate, and came back down to 39°C at IRONMAN pace. He read that as a sign he "was ready for the heat" (The Triathlon Hour).

## What does the research say about his heat prep?

**Hot baths and sauna.** In a controlled trial, 6 days of 40-minute hot baths after runs improved a 5 km test in 33°C by 4.9%, with no change at 18°C ([Zurawlew et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12638)), a small study of 17 men. If you have a heart condition, high or low blood pressure, take blood pressure medication, are pregnant, or have had heat illness, talk to your doctor before trying hot baths or sauna.

**Training in the heat.** An expert consensus recommends repeated exercise in the heat over 1 to 2 weeks, and using cooling during the event ([Racinais et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-094915)). A meta-analysis of 96 articles found the biggest adaptations after more than 14 days ([Tyler et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-016-0538-5)).

**Keeping it to race day.** About 2.5% of heat adaptations are lost for every day without heat ([Daanen et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0808-x)). His heat chamber work 3 weeks out kept heat going close to the race. More in our article on [how long heat acclimation takes](https://raceproof.ai/blog/heat-acclimation-how-long).

**Weighing in.** He weighed himself "before and after every big sessions" to track fluid loss (The Triathlon Hour). After 8 days of heat training, sweat rate rose 0.23 to 0.42 L an hour in 22 trained men ([McDonald et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000004190)).

## How did he fuel and pace in the heat?

He said he took 40 g of carbs right before the bike, "around 145" g an hour on the bike and "around 100 grams an hour" on the run, "which is what we practiced in training". He planned 1.4 to 1.5 L of fluid an hour on the bike and took "1.6 or 1.7" (All-In Triathlon). He used IRONMAN Frankfurt and Boise to keep "practicing nutrition" (ProTriNews). His nutrition partner is 6d Sports Nutrition.

At the aid stations, IRONMAN's own race video shows him drinking one cup of water, pouring a second inside his suit and putting ice in his cap. "I was taking everything I could," he said (The Triathlon Hour).

His pacing plan was patience: "I really wanted to have two matches left for the run" (ProTriNews). In a review of 70.3 studies, conservative bike pacing that spares glycogen was linked to a better final run ([von Känel-Cordoba et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0352506)), an observed link, not proof.

## What it means for you

**What carries over.** The order and timing. Heat has its own window: build it 6 to 2 weeks out, then keep some heat to race day. Gut training starts about 12 weeks out, with race-level carbs by 4 to 6 weeks out.

**What doesn't.** A heat chamber near home, altitude camps and elite physiology. His 145 g/h is not a target. Guidelines set up to 90 g/h for efforts over 2.5 to 3 hours ([2016 position statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26917108/)). A 2026 review says the ceiling could rise to 120 g/h for trained athletes and that higher is unproven ([Morton et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tjnut.2026.101442); several authors have ties to Science in Sport). In 8 elite marathoners taking 120 g/h, gut symptoms peaked ([Ravikanti et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00665.2025), funded by Science in Sport).

## Our take

*This is our opinion, not a study finding.* The useful part of Luis's prep is the order and the timing, not the numbers: heat in stages, race-pace work in the heat in the final weeks, and race fueling practiced long before Kona. Copy that shape at your level, with your own carb target.

### How long did Vincent Luis train in the heat for Kona?
He said he started passive heat (hot baths and sauna) in January and February and added heat training from before IRONMAN Texas in April. He didn't give a total number of sessions or weeks.

### How many carbs did Vincent Luis take at Kona?
About 145 g an hour on the bike and about 100 g an hour on the run, by his own account. Pro intakes are not targets.

### Do I need a heat chamber?
No. Training outside in the heat counts, and so do hot baths ([Racinais et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-094915); [Zurawlew et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12638)). Build up slowly and never alone.
